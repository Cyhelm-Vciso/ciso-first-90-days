# The CISO's First 90 Days

**A free, practical playbook with ready-to-use templates for new CISOs, heads of security and vCISOs. Learn the business, check you can survive an incident, find the real risks, deliver early wins, and walk into your first board meeting with a funded plan.**

## The plan at a glance

| Phase | Goal | Key outputs |
|---|---|---|
| **[Before day 1](playbook/00-before-day-one.md)** | Do the homework, agree the mandate | Business, regulator and leadership research; reporting line, budget and authority confirmed |
| **[Days 1–30: Listen and learn](playbook/01-days-1-30-listen-and-learn.md)** | Understand the business, build relationships, and be incident-ready from week 1 | Incident readiness checked; 20+ stakeholder interviews; crown jewels identified |
| **[Days 31–60: Assess and prioritise](playbook/02-days-31-60-assess-and-prioritise.md)** | An evidence-based current state, the top risks, and visible quick wins | Maturity scores; baseline metrics; top 10 risks agreed with owners; 3–5 quick wins |
| **[Days 61–90: Plan and deliver](playbook/03-days-61-90-plan-and-deliver.md)** | A funded strategy and a governance rhythm | Strategy on a page; costed roadmap with options; board briefing |

Different situation? See **[adapting the playbook](playbook/04-adapting-to-your-situation.md)** for arriving mid-breach, being the first-ever security leader, inheriting a mature programme, regulated sectors, vCISO engagements, and SOC 2 / ISO 27001 scale-ups.

## The first question to answer in week 1

> **"If we got hit by ransomware tonight, what would happen?"**

Who would you call? Is there an IR retainer? What does the insurance policy require, and by when? When was a critical system last restored from backup? Who can authorise shutting things down? If nobody knows, that's your first job, before the listening tour, before the assessment, before anything else.

## Principles

1. **Incident-ready first, strategy second.** You may not get 90 quiet days.
2. **Listen before you change.** The first month is for learning, not for reorganising or buying tools.
3. **Evidence over opinion.** "We have MFA" isn't evidence. An enrolment report is.
4. **Business language.** Risks have business owners and business impacts. The board decides between risks, not between tools.
5. **Earn trust with quick wins.** Small, visible, risk-reducing changes that make someone's life easier.
6. **Fewer, bigger bets.** Four to six strategic initiatives in year one is plenty.
7. **Never criticise your predecessor.** Everything made sense to someone at the time.
