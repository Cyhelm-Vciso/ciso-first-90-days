# Before Day One

The weeks between accepting the offer and starting are the cheapest time to learn. Nobody expects anything from you yet, and people will talk to you openly.

## Do your homework

- [ ] **Understand the business model.** How does the company make money? What would stop it making money for a week? Read the annual report, investor materials, the website and recent press.
- [ ] **Know the regulators.** Data protection, sector regulators (financial, health, critical infrastructure), and regimes such as NIS2, DORA, HIPAA, PCI DSS or SEC disclosure rules. Which ones bite?
- [ ] **Check the public footprint.** Look for past breaches in the news, any public bug bounty or vulnerability disclosure policy, security pages, and certifications claimed (SOC 2, ISO 27001). Public certificates tell you what has been promised to customers.
- [ ] **Map the leadership team.** Who are the CEO, CFO, CIO/CTO, General Counsel and Head of HR? Who chairs the audit or risk committee? What are their backgrounds and priorities?
- [ ] **Find out why the role exists.** Is it new, a replacement, a response to an incident, or driven by a customer or regulator? This shapes everything.

## Agree the mandate (before you sign, or immediately after)

Ask your hiring manager, ideally in writing:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Who do I report to, and do I have direct access to the CEO and the board or audit committee? | Independence and influence |
| What is my budget, and who owns the security budget today? | Whether you can fix anything in year one |
| What team do I inherit, and can I hire? | Capacity |
| What does success look like at 90 days and at 12 months? | Avoid mismatched expectations |
| What decisions can I make alone, and which need approval? | Authority to act in an incident |
| Are there commitments already made: audits, certifications, customer deadlines? | Hidden deadlines |
| What happened to my predecessor? | Context, and landmines |

## Prepare yourself

- [ ] Draft a one-paragraph **personal operating principles** note: how you like to work, how to reach you, and what you'll ask of people. It becomes part of your introduction email.
- [ ] Prepare your **stakeholder interview guide** (see [template](../templates/stakeholder-interview-guide.md)).
- [ ] Line up a few **peer CISOs** you can call. The first 90 days are lonely.
- [ ] Rest. You'll be drinking from a fire hose.

## Red flags to probe now, not later

- "Security reports to IT operations, and the CIO wants to keep it that way."
- No budget line for security, or it's buried in IT.
- A certification audit or major customer security review due within your first 60 days.
- A recent incident that "was handled" but has no written post-incident review.
- Leadership sees the role as purely technical ("fix the firewalls").

None of these is a reason to walk away. They're things to name early and plan for.
