# Adapting the playbook to your situation

The default 30/60/90 rhythm assumes a reasonably stable organisation. Adjust it if one of these describes your situation.

## You arrive during or right after a breach

- Days 1–14 are **incident leadership**, not listening. Stabilise, contain, and support the people who are exhausted.
- Get the facts on the record: a timeline, root cause, notification decisions and open actions.
- Run a blameless post-incident review within the first month. It doubles as your current-state assessment for the affected areas.
- Expect more budget appetite now than you'll ever have again. Ask for what's needed to prevent a repeat, and present it as a plan, not a shopping list.

## You are the first-ever security leader

- There is no programme to assess, so you'll be designing one. Start with the basics that stop most attacks: MFA, patching, backups, endpoint protection and email security.
- Expect to be doing the work as well as leading it. Be explicit about what you **won't** get to in year one.
- Write down the risk decisions leadership makes. Accepted risks are fine; undocumented ones aren't.

## You inherit a mature programme

- Your risk is breaking what works. Lengthen the listening phase and focus on the gaps between teams.
- Look for the "unknown unknowns": shadow IT, acquisitions, OT environments, AI adoption and supplier concentration.
- Your quick wins may be about **removing** friction, such as retiring duplicate tools or simplifying exception processes.

## You work in a regulated sector (financial services, health, critical infrastructure)

- In week 1, map regulatory deadlines and commitments (for example DORA, NIS2, HIPAA or sector supervisors). Find out whether any regulator findings are open.
- Learn the regulatory incident-reporting obligations and clocks before you need them.
- Involve compliance and internal audit early; they can be your strongest allies.

## You are a fractional or virtual CISO (vCISO)

- Compress the plan. Run interviews in two weeks and deliver the assessment by day 30.
- Agree **scope and hours** precisely, including what happens in an incident outside your hours.
- Make sure there's a named internal owner for day-to-day security. Your job is to make them successful.
- Leave artefacts behind (policies, a risk register, a roadmap) that stay useful after your engagement ends.

## You are joining a scale-up preparing for SOC 2 or ISO 27001

- The audit is your forcing function. Align quick wins and policies with the controls the auditor will test.
- Don't let the audit become the strategy. Certification shows customers you have controls; it doesn't make you secure on its own.
- Automate evidence collection early. It saves months later.
