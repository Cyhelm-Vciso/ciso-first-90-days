# Days 1–30: Listen and learn

**Goal:** understand the business, build relationships, and make sure you could lead an incident response tomorrow. **Change almost nothing** except the urgent.

## Week 1: Make sure you can handle a crisis

You may not have long. Before anything else, answer one question: **"If we got hit by ransomware tonight, what would happen?"**

- [ ] Find the incident response plan, if there is one. Who is on it, and are the phone numbers current?
- [ ] Do we have an **incident response retainer** or MDR provider? Get the contact details and contract into your phone.
- [ ] Do we have **cyber insurance**? Read the notification requirements. Many policies require notice within hours to days and specify approved responders.
- [ ] When was the last **successful restore test** of critical systems? Are backups immutable or offline?
- [ ] Who has **authority** to shut down systems, engage external responders, and notify regulators?
- [ ] Get **break-glass** access for yourself, or know who holds it.

If the answers are "nobody knows", fixing that is your first quick win (see [Days 31–60](02-days-31-60-assess-and-prioritise.md)).

Also in week 1:
- [ ] Send your [introduction email](../templates/introduction-email.md)
- [ ] Meet your manager to confirm the 30/60/90-day expectations in writing
- [ ] Meet your team one-to-one: their role, their worries, what they'd fix first, and what they need from you
- [ ] Book all stakeholder interviews for weeks 2–4

## Weeks 2–4: The listening tour

Interview **20–30 people** using the [stakeholder interview guide](../templates/stakeholder-interview-guide.md). Track them on the [stakeholder map](../templates/stakeholder-map.md).

| Group | Who | What you're learning |
|---|---|---|
| Executives | CEO, CFO, COO, CIO/CTO, General Counsel, CHRO, CRO | Strategy, risk appetite, what keeps them up at night, how they want to hear from you |
| Board | Audit or risk committee chair (with your manager's blessing) | What the board expects, and how security has been reported before |
| Business units | Heads of sales, operations, product and customer success | Crown jewels, friction caused by security, upcoming initiatives |
| Technology | Infrastructure, cloud, engineering, service desk, architecture | How things really work, technical debt, shadow IT |
| Risk and assurance | Internal audit, compliance, privacy/DPO, legal, procurement | Open findings, regulatory commitments, contract obligations |
| Outside | Key customers' security teams (via account managers), MSSP/MDR, auditors, peers | How the company is seen from outside |

**Ask everyone the same three questions.** The patterns are the insight:
1. What are we most worried about, security-wise?
2. Where does security get in the way of you doing your job?
3. If you were me, what would you do first?

## Gather the evidence

Collect existing documents. Don't judge them yet; just collect.

- [ ] Policies and standards, and their approval dates
- [ ] Previous risk assessments and the risk register
- [ ] Audit, pentest and certification reports, with open findings
- [ ] Security budget, contracts and renewal dates (tools, MSSP, insurance)
- [ ] Architecture diagrams, asset inventories, cloud accounts
- [ ] Incident history for the last 2–3 years
- [ ] Customer security commitments (contracts, questionnaires, trust centre)
- [ ] Board and committee papers on security from the last 12–24 months

## Outputs by day 30

- [ ] Incident readiness gaps identified, and the most dangerous ones fixed or escalated
- [ ] 20+ stakeholder interviews done, with themes written up
- [ ] Draft list of crown-jewel assets and critical business services
- [ ] Initial observations (not conclusions) shared with your manager

## Pitfalls

- **Criticising your predecessor or the team.** Everything had a reason at the time. You'll need these people.
- **Announcing a reorganisation or a new tool.** You don't know enough yet.
- **Only talking to IT.** Security risk is business risk; the business sets the priorities.
- **Disappearing into the technical weeds.** Diving into firewall rules in week 2 feels productive but isn't your job.
