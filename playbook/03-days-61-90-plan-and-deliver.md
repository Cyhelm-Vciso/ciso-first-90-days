# Days 61–90: Plan and deliver

**Goal:** agree a funded strategy with leadership, establish how security will be governed and reported, and present to the board.

## Week 9–10: Strategy and roadmap

Write the [security strategy on a page](../templates/security-strategy-on-a-page.md):

1. **Business context:** what we're protecting and why it matters (crown jewels, regulators, customer commitments)
2. **Where we are:** maturity scores and top risks, in plain language
3. **Where we need to be:** a 12–36 month target state tied to business goals (for example "pass the SOC 2 audit to unlock enterprise deals", or "recover from ransomware within 24 hours")
4. **How we'll get there:** 4–6 strategic initiatives, each with an outcome, owner, cost and timeline
5. **What we need:** budget, headcount and decisions
6. **How we'll measure it:** 5 key risk indicators with targets

Build the **roadmap** in three horizons:

| Horizon | Timeframe | Typical content |
|---|---|---|
| Now | Next 3–6 months | Close critical gaps: identity, backups, critical vulnerabilities, incident readiness |
| Next | 6–18 months | Build the programme: detection and response coverage, supplier risk, secure development, certification |
| Later | 18–36 months | Optimise: automation, zero trust, advanced detection, resilience testing |

Price each initiative with a range, and say what happens if it **isn't** funded. Executives decide between risks, not between tools.

## Week 10–11: Governance and operating model

- [ ] **Policies:** agree which policies must be written or refreshed, and the approval route (see [security-policy-templates](https://github.com/Cyhelm-Vciso/security-policy-templates))
- [ ] **Risk governance:** who accepts which level of risk, and how often the top risks are reviewed
- [ ] **Reporting rhythm:** monthly to the executive team and quarterly to the board or committee
- [ ] **Team structure:** roles you have, roles you need, and build / buy / outsource decisions
- [ ] **RACI** for key processes: incident response, vulnerability management, access reviews, supplier assessments
- [ ] **Security champions** in engineering and business units, if your culture supports it

## Week 12–13: The day-90 briefing

Present to the executive team, then the board or committee, using the [90-day board briefing](../templates/90-day-board-briefing.md) template:

1. What I found: a balanced picture, including what's good
2. Top risks, and which are outside appetite
3. What we've already improved (quick wins with numbers)
4. The plan, the cost, and the decisions needed
5. How you'll hear from me from now on

Rehearse with your manager and at least one friendly executive first. **No surprises in the boardroom**: every risk owner should already have seen their risk.

## Outputs by day 90

- [ ] Strategy on a page and roadmap, endorsed by the executive team
- [ ] Budget and headcount request submitted, with costed options
- [ ] Top-risk register owned by the business, with a review cadence
- [ ] Reporting rhythm and metrics agreed
- [ ] Board or committee briefed
- [ ] Your own 12-month objectives agreed with your manager

## Pitfalls

- **A "boil the ocean" roadmap.** Four to six initiatives are plenty for year one.
- **Asking for money without options.** Offer good, better and best options, with the risk each one leaves.
- **Making the board briefing a technology tour.** Talk about business impact, trend and decisions.
- **Stopping the listening.** Keep one-to-ones with key stakeholders on a regular cadence.

## After day 90

The first 90 days build credibility. Spend it on delivery: the "Now" horizon initiatives, monthly metrics, and your first annual cycle of policy review, risk assessment and incident exercise.
